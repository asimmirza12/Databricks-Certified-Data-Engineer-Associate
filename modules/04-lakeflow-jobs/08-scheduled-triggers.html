Scheduled triggers

The simplest reason for a job to run is the clock: at two in the morning, every weekday at half past seven, once a month. A scheduled trigger starts a run at the times you set, written as a Quartz cron expression.

The idea

A timetable for the job

A scheduled trigger starts a job's run at times you choose. You do not press Run; the clock does.

The times are written as a Quartz cron expression. It has six fields, separated by spaces, and they are read left to right:

seconds minutes hours day-of-month month day-of-week

Two rules trip people up. First, Quartz starts with seconds, unlike the five-field cron on many Unix systems. Second, day-of-month and day-of-week cannot both be set: one of them must be a question mark ?, which means "no value".

A schedule also has a timezone. The times are interpreted in that zone, so the same expression can mean different moments for a team in another country.

Everyday analogy

A bin collection timetable. "Every Tuesday at 7 am" is written once, and the lorry comes without anyone asking. Quartz is the timetable's notation: seconds, minutes, hours, then the date parts. The question mark is the timetable saying "not this part": the bins go on Tuesday, whatever the day of the month.

How it works

Reading the six fields

The fields are read as one moment: the schedule runs when all six match.

Choosing the timezone

The timezone is part of the schedule. "07:30" in London is a different moment from "07:30" in Los Angeles. Set it to the zone where the business runs, so reports are ready when people arrive, and remember that clocks change with daylight saving.

Pausing and starting

A schedule can be paused without being deleted. A paused schedule starts no runs until it is resumed. Manual runs still work while the schedule is paused.

Code

  
A schedule in a job


name: ingest_bakery_nightly
schedule:
  quartz_cron_expression: "0 0 2 * * ?"      # 02:00 every day
  timezone_id: "Europe/London"            # read the time in London
  pause_status: "UNPAUSED"                 # set to PAUSED to stop scheduled runs
tasks:
  - task_key: load_orders
    notebook_task: {notebook_path: /Workspace/bakery/load_orders}

  
The field names above are the bundle form as the docs show them. Check the exact names, and whether your workspace uses a different schedule form, in the job reference before use.

Don't confuse these

  
Schedule traps

  

    
Quartz vs Unix cron
Quartz has six fields, with seconds first. Unix cron has five, with minutes first.

    
? vs *
* means every value. ? means no value, and is used in whichever of day-of-month or day-of-week is not set.

    
Paused vs deleted
A paused schedule keeps its settings and can be resumed. A deleted one is gone.

    
Scheduled vs triggered
A scheduled trigger runs on the clock. A file arrival or table update trigger runs on the data (lessons 04-09 and 04-10).

  






  
Check yourself

  
Quiz

  

    

      1. How many fields does a Quartz cron expression have, and which comes first?
      
      
      
      
Quartz uses six fields: seconds, minutes, hours, day-of-month, month and day-of-week.

    

    

      2. Which expression runs at 07:30 on weekdays only?
      
      
      
      
Day-of-month is ? because weekday is used. The minute, hour and seconds give 07:30:00.

    

    

      3. In the demo, the same expression was shown in two timezones. What does the timezone change?
      
      
      
      
The fields are the same. The timezone decides which clock they are read against.

    

    

      4. What does pausing a schedule do?
      
      
      
      
Pausing keeps the schedule's settings and stops only the automatic starts.
