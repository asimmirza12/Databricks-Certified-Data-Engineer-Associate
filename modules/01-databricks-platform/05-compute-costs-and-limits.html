Compute costs and limits: DBUs and what each option cannot do

Every kind of compute has a price and a list of things it will not do. Know both, and choosing becomes simple arithmetic.

The idea

You pay for time switched on, measured in DBUs

Databricks does not charge by the gigabyte of data or by the query. It charges for compute that is running. The unit of measurement is the DBU, short for Databricks Unit. A DBU is a standard measure of processing power used for one hour. A bigger machine uses more DBUs per hour than a smaller one.

Your charge from Databricks is:

DBUs used × the price of one DBU.

The price of one DBU is not a single number. It depends on which kind of compute produced it. Each kind has its own price label, called a SKU. A DBU from all-purpose compute costs noticeably more than a DBU from jobs compute. That gap is the reason scheduled work should not run on all-purpose compute.

There is a second charge to remember, and it depends on where the machines live (lesson 01-01):

  • Classic compute uses machines in your own cloud account. So you get two bills: DBUs from Databricks, and the machines themselves from your cloud provider.
  • Serverless compute uses machines in the Databricks account. You get one bill: the serverless DBU price already includes the machines.
Everyday analogy

Electricity. You are billed for units used, not for owning appliances. A kettle burns more units per hour than a lamp, just as a large cluster burns more DBUs per hour than a small one. Leave the lights on in an empty house and you still pay: that is idle compute. And some tariffs are dearer than others: the "convenience" tariff for all-purpose compute costs more per unit than the "off-peak" tariff for jobs compute.

How it works

One bill or two

Comparing only the DBU price of classic and serverless is misleading. Add the cloud provider's machine charge to the classic side first.

Where idle time hides

With classic compute you pay from the moment the machines start until they stop, including the minutes spent starting and any time they sit idle. With serverless you are billed only while compute is assigned to your work, not for the time taken to get machines ready. This is why a pricier serverless DBU can still give a smaller total bill for short or bursty work.

Seeing what was spent

Usage is recorded in system tables. system.billing.usage holds one row per slice of usage, with the SKU and the quantity of DBUs. system.billing.list_prices holds the list price of each SKU. Join the two to turn DBUs into money. You can also attach tags (labels such as team=finance) to compute, so the usage rows show who the cost belongs to.

Code

Reading your own usage

These queries need access to the system catalog, which an administrator grants.

-- DBUs used per day and per SKU over the last week.
SELECT usage_date,
       sku_name,
       SUM(usage_quantity) AS dbus
FROM system.billing.usage
WHERE usage_date >= date_sub(current_date(), 7)
GROUP BY usage_date, sku_name
ORDER BY usage_date, dbus DESC;
-- Turn DBUs into an estimated cost at list price, split by a team tag.
SELECT u.custom_tags['team']                        AS team,
       SUM(u.usage_quantity * p.pricing.default)    AS list_cost
FROM system.billing.usage AS u
JOIN system.billing.list_prices AS p
  ON  u.sku_name = p.sku_name
  AND u.usage_start_time >= p.price_start_time
  AND (p.price_end_time IS NULL OR u.usage_start_time < p.price_end_time)
WHERE u.usage_date >= date_sub(current_date(), 30)
GROUP BY u.custom_tags['team'];

List price is the published price. What your company actually pays may differ under its contract. For classic compute this figure also leaves out the cloud provider's machine charges, which appear on the cloud provider's bill. Check column names against the billing system table reference before relying on them.

What if I change this?

What moves the bill

ChangeEffect
Move a nightly job from all-purpose to jobs computeThe same DBUs are charged at a much lower price per DBU. Nothing else changes.
Shorten auto termination from 120 minutes to 20Up to 100 fewer idle minutes are billed after each working session.
Double the size of a clusterTwice the DBUs and machine cost per hour. If the job finishes in half the time, the total is about the same. If it does not, you paid for nothing.
Move short, frequent jobs to serverlessNo start-up minutes and no idle time are billed. Often cheaper overall, even at a higher DBU price.
Use spot machines for classic workersSpot machines are spare capacity that cloud providers sell cheaply and can take back at short notice. The cloud bill drops. A run may be slowed if machines are reclaimed.
Add tags to computeNo change to cost. But each usage row now says which team or project it belongs to.

What each option cannot do

Serverless (notebooks and jobs)

No R. No Scala in notebooks. No RDD API. Only a short list of Spark settings can be changed. Streaming must use the availableNow trigger. Libraries are installed per notebook, not per compute. A single run can last at most 7 days.

SQL warehouse

SQL only. No Python, Scala or R. You choose a size, not individual machines or Spark settings.

All-purpose (classic)

Takes minutes to start. Costs the most per DBU. Bills idle time. With Standard access mode: no R and no RDD API.

Jobs compute (classic)

Takes minutes to start, on every run. Cannot be used interactively. Gone when the run ends, so nothing can be inspected on it afterwards.

Limits change as the product develops, serverless most of all. Treat this list as a snapshot and check the serverless limitations page before an exam or a design decision.

Don't confuse these

Cost traps

DBU vs money

A DBU is a unit of usage, not a currency. The same number of DBUs costs different amounts on different SKUs.

Databricks bill vs cloud bill

Classic compute appears on two bills. Serverless appears on one. Exam questions about "total cost" expect you to remember the second bill.

Auto termination vs autoscaling

Auto termination switches compute off when idle. Autoscaling changes the number of workers while it runs. Both save money in different ways.

Cheaper DBU vs cheaper job

A lower price per DBU helps only if the DBU count stays similar. Idle time and start-up time can outweigh it.

Check yourself

Quiz

1. What is a DBU?

DBUs measure compute usage. Money comes from multiplying DBUs by the price of the SKU.

2. A scheduled job runs on all-purpose compute. What is the simplest way to cut its cost?

Jobs compute has a much lower price per DBU than all-purpose compute, for the same work.

3. Which compute produces a single bill that already includes the machines?

Serverless machines belong to Databricks, so their cost is inside the serverless DBU price. Classic machines are billed by your cloud provider.

4. In the demo, what happened to the all-purpose bill as "hours left on but idle" went up?

Only compute that stays on between pieces of work bills idle time. Jobs compute ends with the run, and serverless bills only assigned work.

5. Where do you look to see DBU usage per SKU?

The billing system tables record usage. The other two describe tables, not spending.

Wrap up

Key takeaways

  • Cost = DBUs used × the price per DBU of that compute's SKU.
  • All-purpose compute has the highest classic price per DBU. Jobs compute is much cheaper.
  • Classic compute means two bills. Serverless means one, with machines included.
  • Idle time and start-up time are billed on classic compute, not on serverless.
  • Serverless and SQL warehouses trade flexibility for simplicity. Know their limits.
