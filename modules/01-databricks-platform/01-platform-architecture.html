Platform architecture: control plane and compute plane

Databricks is split into two halves. One half is the brain that Databricks runs. The other half is the muscle that touches your data. Knowing which is which answers many exam questions.

The idea

A brain that manages, and muscle that processes

Lesson 00-07 showed three things: the workspace, compute and storage. Now we ask a sharper question: whose cloud account does each part run in? There are two accounts involved. One belongs to Databricks. One belongs to your company.

  • The control plane is the set of services that Databricks runs in its own account. It serves the web pages you click on, stores your notebooks, schedules jobs and starts and stops compute. It manages. It does not crunch your data.
  • The compute plane is where your data is actually processed. This is where Spark's driver and executors run.

The compute plane comes in two kinds, and the difference is simply where the machines live:

  • The classic compute plane is in your cloud account. The machines are created inside your company's own network.
  • The serverless compute plane is in the Databricks account, in the same region as your workspace. Databricks owns and runs the machines.
Everyday analogy

A catering company. The head office takes bookings, plans menus and sends out staff rotas. That is the control plane. The cooking can happen in two places. With classic compute, the caterer's chefs come and cook in your own kitchen: your building, your ovens, their recipes. With serverless, the food is cooked in the caterer's kitchen and brought to you: nothing for you to set up, and it arrives quickly. Either way the ingredients, your data, belong to you and are kept in your pantry.

Account and workspaces

One more level sits above all this. Your company has one Databricks account. Inside it you create one or more workspaces, for example one for development and one for production. Each workspace lives in one cloud region. Users, groups and Unity Catalog are managed at the account level, so they can be shared by several workspaces.

Workspace storage

A classic workspace also has a small storage bucket of its own in your cloud account, called workspace storage. It keeps the workspace's own housekeeping files, such as job results and logs. It is separate from the storage that holds your tables and volumes. Do not delete it: the docs warn that the workspace cannot be recovered if you do.

Asking the platform where you are

Architecture is mostly about layout, not code. But a few built-in SQL functions tell you which parts of the platform your session is connected to. They are handy at the top of a notebook.

-- Who am I, and which catalog and schema am I pointed at?
SELECT current_user()      AS me,
       current_catalog()   AS catalog_in_use,
       current_schema()    AS schema_in_use;

-- Which Unity Catalog metastore is this workspace attached to?
SELECT current_metastore();

The same from Python, in any notebook:

row = spark.sql("SELECT current_user() AS me, current_catalog() AS cat").first()
print(row.me, row.cat)   # this code runs on the compute plane, not in your browser
What if I change this?

Classic or serverless: what moves

ChangeEffect
Switch a notebook from classic to serverless computeThe machines doing the work move from your cloud account to the Databricks account. Your tables stay exactly where they were.
Stop every compute resourceThe control plane still works. You can log in, edit notebooks and browse the catalog. You cannot run code or query data.
Add a second workspace in the same accountIt gets its own notebooks, jobs and compute. It can share users, groups and Unity Catalog data with the first one.
Use classic computeYou see the virtual machines on your cloud provider's bill and in your own network, and you choose their size and settings.
Delete the workspace storage bucketThe workspace breaks and cannot be recovered. Your Unity Catalog tables are in different storage and are not in that bucket.

What lives where

Control plane

The web application, notebook and query text, job definitions and schedules, and the service that launches compute.

Compute plane

Spark drivers and executors, and SQL warehouses. Anything that reads or writes rows.

Your storage

The Parquet files and logs of your Delta tables, and the files in your volumes.

Don't confuse these

Layout words

Control plane vs compute plane

Control manages. Compute processes. If a question is about where data is crunched, the answer is the compute plane.

Classic vs serverless plane

Classic: your cloud account. Serverless: the Databricks account. This one-line difference is the most likely thing to be tested.

Compute plane vs data plane

Older material says "data plane". It means the same thing as compute plane, which is the current name.

Account vs workspace

One account holds many workspaces. Users, groups and Unity Catalog belong to the account. Notebooks, jobs and compute belong to a workspace.

Check yourself

Quiz

1. Where do the machines of the classic compute plane run?

Classic compute is created inside your company's cloud account and network. Serverless compute is the kind that runs in the Databricks account.

2. Which of these is a job of the control plane?

The control plane manages and coordinates. Executors run in a compute plane, and table files sit in your storage.

3. In the demo, all compute was stopped. What could you still do?

Opening a notebook only needs the control plane. Anything that touches data needs a compute plane.

4. A notebook is moved from classic to serverless compute. What happens to the table it reads?

Storage is separate from compute. Changing where the processing happens does not move the data.

Wrap up

Key takeaways

  • The control plane manages. The compute plane processes data.
  • The control plane is always in the Databricks account.
  • Classic compute runs in your cloud account. Serverless compute runs in the Databricks account.
  • Your data stays in your storage, whichever compute you use.
  • One account holds many workspaces. Each workspace is in one region.
